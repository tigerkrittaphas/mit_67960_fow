# Train advisors on the principal, not themselves

**As literally proposed, the design is unsound as a path to playing strength, but a modified version is worth running as a scientific experiment.** The proposal trains separate deep-RL advisors greedily on their own rewards, sums their per-move Q-values, samples from a softmax over that sum, and fits the weights afterwards by NNLS or CMA-ES. Four problems undermine it, and each has direct evidence. First, Collina et al.'s theorems assume strategic advisors and treat the weighted sum as an assumption about utilities, whereas here the principal computes the sum itself against an adapting opponent. Second, independently greedy advisors are known to produce optimistic, policy-inconsistent sums that create do-nothing "attractor" states (Russell & Zimdars; Laroche et al.). Third, a softmax over a fixed score is a product of experts, not an equilibrium, and Obscuro beat its own unrestricted-mixing variant 70.2%. Fourth, every strong dark-chess system wins through belief tracking and game-theoretic search rather than through a better evaluation: Obscuro running only a material-plus-visibility evaluation still beat the earlier ~2000-strength bot. The weighted sum of advisor Q-values equals the principal's own value, for any weights, only if every advisor evaluates that same principal policy. The sound version therefore trains advisors "empathically", with expected-Sarsa targets under the principal's softmax, writes material, king safety and pressure as potential-based shaping, and tunes the remaining weights on win rate. Taken to its conclusion, that sound version collapses into a single win-value learner with auxiliary heads, so Collina's alignment question lives mainly in a cheaper place: a heuristic council fitted Texel-style. That council also fits the 2-core, 4 GiB runtime and the actual course arena. The novel, publishable part is measuring Collina's "market-alignment" ε empirically in a zero-sum imperfect-information game and testing whether a learned combination beats the best single advisor.

## Collina's guarantees do not cover a council that computes its own sum

The motivation borrows the right object in the wrong role. Collina, Goel, Roth, Ryu and Shi define **ε-market alignment** as the existence of nonnegative weights summing to one and an offset c such that **the sup over all actions and states of |Σ w_i U_i(a,y) + c − u_A(a,y)| is at most ε** ([arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15090v2)). Their Theorems 1, 2, 5 and 6 then show that *strategic* AI providers competing in a Stackelberg or persuasion equilibrium deliver near-first-best utility to the user. Theorem 6 (Best-AI Selection) needs nothing beyond ε-alignment and equilibrium play to guarantee **at least U_A(C_B*) − 2ε** ([arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15090v2)).

The council inverts every role. Its advisors are fixed scoring functions with no incentive to compete, the principal computes the weighted sum itself, and the hidden state is produced by an adversary who adapts to the principal rather than drawn by nature from a common prior. None of the equilibrium theorems applies, and the authors themselves call their experiments "very simple and stylized" ([arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15090v2)). The repository's own handoff note reaches the same conclusion and treats the theorems as motivation only (`docs/handoff-council-experiment.md`).

What transfers literally is Section 6, which is constrained stacked regression. Over 100 prompt-varied LLM "advisors", **NNLS weights cut MSE relative to the best single advisor by about 52% on ETHICS and 75% on MovieLens**, with only about 18–26 nonzero weights, while "the simple average performs poorly (even worse than the best individual in MovieLens)" ([arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15090v2)).

Porting that to chess carries three caveats. First, Collina et al. regress against observed true utility, so the council needs an equivalent label: the game outcome, a stronger engine's value, or win rate under CMA-ES. Second, win probability is roughly logistic in a linear evaluation, so the fit belongs in logit space. That makes NNLS on outcomes **Texel tuning with a nonnegativity constraint**: Texel's method is "a concrete instance of a logistic regression" and brought one engine about +100 Elo ([Chessprogramming wiki](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Texel%27s_Tuning_Method)). Third, ε is a uniform bound over every action and hidden state, and FoW chess's king-capture cliffs are exactly where a smooth sum of proxies misses badly.

The paper also puts a price on the softmax. The quantal-response loss is **at most log|𝒜|/λ** (Lemma 1), about 3.5/λ in score units for 30–40 legal moves, and that cost applies even in the paper's cooperative setting ([arXiv v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15090v2)).

## Greedy advisors summed together inherit the illusion of control

The reward-decomposition literature has studied this exact architecture, and its verdict turns on one question: which policy does each advisor evaluate?

Russell and Zimdars' arbitrator "selects an action maximizing the sum of Q-values from all the subagents." Subagents that run local Q-learning against their own greedy max converge to **"selfish" estimates that "overestimate the true values of their own rewards"**, so the sum is an optimistic upper bound that is not tied to any policy ([Russell & Zimdars 2003](https://aima.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/ml03-qdecomp.pdf)). In their dollar/euro example, once γ exceeds 0.5 the summed selfish values prefer doing nothing forever over collecting any reward. In a 10-boat fishery, selfish subagents produced a tragedy of the commons that "crashed within a couple of years." Local Sarsa, which bootstraps on the action the arbitrator actually took, provably converges to the globally optimal decomposition.

Laroche et al. formalise three regimes ([Multi-Advisor RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00756)):

- **Egocentric:** each advisor evaluates its own greedy policy. This overestimates values and creates **attractor** states where staying put beats progress toward any single advisor's goal.
- **Agnostic:** advisors evaluate the random policy. This avoids attractors but is risk-averse and of near-random quality.
- **Empathic:** advisors evaluate the aggregator's policy. This converges to the global optimum when advisors see the full state.

In Pac-Boy, egocentric advisors with γ = 0.9 drove the agent to the centre, where it "does not move until a ghost comes too close." Empathic planning stayed robust under reward noise with 100 times larger variance.

Chess is attractor-prone because it has many quiet waiting moves and its advisors disagree constantly. Material wants to grab, king safety wants to consolidate, and scouting wants to probe. Each egocentric advisor assumes its own plan remains available next turn, so the sum favours noncommittal shuffling.

Hybrid Reward Architecture, the flagship success for summed heads, supports greedy advisors less than it appears to. Its Ms. Pac-Man score of 25,304 (against 15,693 for humans) used heads that evaluated the **random policy**, not their own greedy policy. Head weights were set by hand to in-game point values, including a hand-tuned −1,000 for ghosts, across about 1,800 general value functions in a navigation domain ([van Seijen et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04208)). The authors concede that the greedy-max target is "generally not consistent," and random-policy values are meaningless in chess, where random continuations drop pieces.

The off-policy fix is drQ. It computes the overall greedy action a⁺ = argmax Σ_c Q_c(s′,a) and updates every component toward r_c + γQ_c(s′,a⁺), which in the tabular case recovers both the optimal policy and correct component values ([Juozapaitis et al. 2019](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~afern/papers/reward_decomposition__workshop_final.pdf)).

**The cleanest way to see the issue is through successor features.** If each advisor's reward is a feature φ_j and every advisor evaluates the same policy π, the advisor Q-vector is ψ^π, and **Q^π_w = ψ^π · w exactly, for every w** ([Barreto et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.05312)). This is the only condition under which "weighted sum of advisor Q-values" means "the value of the principal's policy," and egocentric training breaks it.

For a softmax principal, the corresponding advisor target is r_j + γ Σ_a π_w(a|s′) Q_j(s′,a), using the principal's current weights. Refitting w on frozen advisors then leaves those advisors evaluating a stale policy. Multi-objective RL handles this by conditioning a vector-valued Q on w ([Abels et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.07803)) or with envelope Q-learning ([Yang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08342)).

Linear scalarization has a further limit: it only reaches the convex coverage set, so Pareto-optimal policies in concave regions of the front are unreachable for any w ([Yang et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.08342)). A win-optimal policy that trades material against king safety differently by phase may need state-dependent weights. Texel tuning's +10.2 Elo from separate middlegame and endgame terms points the same way ([Chessprogramming wiki](https://www.chessprogramming.org/Texel%27s_Tuning_Method)).

**Shaping theory then splits the advisors in two, and following that split exposes what the sound council really is.** Potential-based shaping, F = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s), is the *only* additive shaping that preserves optimal policies ([Ng, Harada & Russell 1999](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/icml99-shaping.pdf)), and in stochastic games it preserves the Nash equilibria ([Devlin & Kudenko 2011](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/75111/)).

Material, king safety and pressure can all be written as potentials. An empathically evaluated potential-based advisor's Q-value collapses to roughly −Φ(s) plus a terminal correction, and that term is the same for every action at a given state. In the exact case it therefore carries no information for move choice, the greedy policy is independent of w, and NNLS has nothing real to discover. This is an inference from the shaping theory, not a tested result. These advisors earn their keep only through what they do to learning: faster credit assignment, auxiliary representation learning, and lower approximation error.

The sound council therefore reduces to **a single win-value learner with auxiliary heads**. Its weights matter in only two places: the scouting/information-gain head, which is not naturally a potential and genuinely changes the objective (so tune it on win rate, not by regression), and the function-approximation regime, where heads are imperfect.

Two practical warnings follow. **If the terminal-win head is among the NNLS regressors and the target is win value, it will absorb nearly all the weight**, which tells you nothing about the others. And proxy sums are gameable. Skalse et al. show that two nontrivial rewards are essentially never "unhackable" relative to each other over stochastic policies ([NeurIPS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13085v1)). A material term placed inside the action-selection sum, rather than used as a shaping potential, invites bait sacrifices in a game where the king is captured, not mated, and is often invisible. That last failure mode is inferred, not observed in any cited study.

## Softmax over a summed Q is a product of experts, not an equilibrium

Algebraically, softmax(λ Σ_i w_i s_i) is proportional to Π_i softmax(λ w_i s_i). The principal is therefore **a weighted product of experts (a log-linear pool), not a mixture**, with each w_i acting as that advisor's inverse temperature. A product is sharper than a mixture because every expert's constraints must be roughly satisfied ([Hinton 2002](https://www.cs.utoronto.ca/~hinton/absps/nccd.pdf)). Any single advisor can veto a move, which suits a blunder-avoiding king-safety advisor and is dangerous for a miscalibrated one.

The closest RL precedent for learned weights is Marivate and Littman, who fit a linear combination of base agents' Q-values by TD toward the environment's return. Their method "compares favorably to the best tuned algorithm" ([AAAI 2013](https://cdn.aaai.org/ocs/ws/ws0960/7025-30741-1-PB.pdf)). Ensembles with Boltzmann combination rules beat single learners on small, partly observable mazes ([Wiering & van Hasselt 2008](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~shivaram/readings/b2hd-WieringVanHasselt2008.html)). None of this work involves an adversary.

In a zero-sum imperfect-information game, the randomness has a specific job to do. A quantal response equilibrium (QRE) is a *fixed point* in which each player's logit response is to the other's equilibrium strategy ([McKelvey & Palfrey 1995](https://resolver.caltech.edu/CaltechAUTHORS:20171128-164515991)). A logit over fixed advisor scores is not that: its move frequencies come from score gaps and λ, not from indifference or bluff value. It can therefore be **exploitable and needlessly noisy at the same time**.

Obscuro's authors state that a FoW agent "must at least somewhat play a mixed strategy," because a known pure strategy reduces the game to ordinary chess for the opponent. Yet full Obscuro mixes only among its top one to three moves, and only when the strategy is judged safe, and it **beat its own unrestricted-mixing variant 70.2% over 1,000 games** ([Zhang & Sandholm 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01242v2)). DeepNash's softmax policy was "slightly distorted by low-probability mistakes" and needed thresholding and discretization ([Perolat et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15378v1.pdf)). The single-GPU PPO agent for Reconnaissance Blind Chess (RBC) found argmax play "slightly better" than sampling ([Bertram et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02029)). Tuning λ and w by CMA-ES against a fixed opponent pool also overfits to that pool, which is the cycling problem R-NaD, MMD and NFSP were designed to avoid ([Perolat et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378); [Sokota et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05825); [Heinrich & Silver 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.01121)).

There is a principled middle ground. Magnetic Mirror Descent is a QRE solver with linear convergence that also works as deep self-play RL in Dark Hex and Phantom Tic-Tac-Toe ([Sokota et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05825)). The council's Σ w_i s_i can serve as its "magnet", the reference policy. That keeps the advisors' prior knowledge and the softmax form while the training dynamics pull the policy toward an equilibrium of the real game. Separately, a Texel-style logistic fit yields the natural scale constant K, which is a principled starting value for λ instead of a guess.

## Every strong dark-chess system wins through belief tracking and search

The state of the art is unanimous: strength comes from tracking the set of possible boards and reasoning game-theoretically over it, not from a clever evaluation.

| System | Core method | Learned components | Strength evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| ZS21 (NeurIPS 2021) | Depth-limited 1-KLSS subgame solving over ≥200 sampled positions; Stockfish 13 depth-11 leaves | None | ~2000 on chess.com FoW (authors' rough estimate); lost 9–1 to a 2416 FM ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2106.06068)) |
| Obscuro (ICLR 2026) | Exact belief set, KLUSS, PCFR+ with one-sided GT-CFR; Stockfish 14 depth-1 MultiPV leaves; safety-gated purification | None | 97% vs humans rated 1450–2006; 16–4 vs the #1 player; 85.1% vs ZS21 ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01242v2)) |
| Simple-eval Obscuro | Same search; evaluation counts only material and visible squares | None | Beat ZS21 55.0% over 10,000 games; scored 18.1% vs full Obscuro ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01242v2)) |
| RBC winners 2019–22 (StrangeFish, penumbra, Fianchetto, StrangeFish2) | Board-set tracking plus Stockfish/Lc0 over sampled boards; sensing chosen for decision value | Mostly none; penumbra uses a ResNet over board-set "synopses" | Won the NeurIPS competitions ([Gardner et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v220/gardner23a/gardner23a.pdf)) |
| RBC SL+PPO agent | Imitation on human games, then PPO self-play, no search; one M40 for a few days | Whole policy | 1118 → 1330 Elo, rank 27 of 84 ([Bertram et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02029)) |
| DeepNash (Stratego) | R-NaD, a Nash-converging model-free RL method | Whole policy | Won 42 of 50 ranked Gravon matches (84%); 3rd on the leaderboard ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.15378v1.pdf)) |

The simple-eval ablation bears most directly on this proposal. Material plus visibility is essentially the proposed material and scouting advisors, and inside belief-aware equilibrium search it was enough to beat a ~2000-strength bot. Evaluation quality still mattered on top of that: full Obscuro beat the simple-eval version 81.9% ([Zhang & Sandholm 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01242v2)). So the binding constraint on the proposed design is the missing belief tracking and search, not reward shaping.

RBC adds a sharper lesson about a standalone scouting reward. Top bots keep the median number of possible boards below about 35, but below that level uncertainty barely correlates with rank. The organisers conclude that **"simply minimizing uncertainty is insufficient. Accounting for the impact of exactly what one will do with an observation appears to be critical."** The one RL-centric RBC entrant, uccchess, did not place near the top. Three top bots beat each other in a cycle, a direct demonstration that heuristic strategies are exploitable ([Gardner et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v220/gardner23a/gardner23a.pdf)).

Determinization, which solves sampled boards as if they were known, suffers from **strategy fusion**: it pretends a different choice can be made in each sampled world. Information-set MCTS mitigates this ([Cowling et al. 2012](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/75048/1/CowlingPowleyWhitehouse2012.pdf)). Advisors trained on true-board rewards inherit a soft version of the same optimism. Determinized engine evaluation with worst-case aggregation is still the practical RBC recipe under tight compute, so it is a strong baseline rather than something to avoid.

Pure RL without search reached expert level in exactly one imperfect-information game, DeepNash, and only through a Nash-converging dynamic ([Perolat et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.15378)). The RBC report notes that NFSP, Deep-CFR, DREAM, ESCHER and R-NaD "have not achieved superhuman play in complex games without search" ([Gardner et al. 2023](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v220/gardner23a/gardner23a.pdf)). ReBeL and Student of Games establish RL plus search over belief states as the principled recipe ([Brown et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13544); [Schmid et al. 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10651118)). Obscuro's authors list a deep-RL-learned evaluation as future work, and that evaluator role is where "advisors" belong.

The verdict still has to be calibrated to the actual venue. The course arena is not Obscuro. Opponents are classmates' bots plus a reference team near 1000 Elo, and the provided baseline captures when it can and otherwise moves at random (`docs/handoff-council-experiment.md`). In that field, a council with belief filtering, a hard king-capture safety check and Texel-fitted heuristic weights may already be strong. "Competitive in this arena" and "competitive with the state of the art" are different bars, and deep-RL advisors are an expensive upgrade aimed at the second.

## The budget fits one shared network, not five independent RL agents

Deployment is not the bottleneck. In local measurements on the project workstation, ONNX Runtime with 2 threads ran an AlphaZero-style residual CNN with a 1×1-conv, 73-plane policy head in **0.36 ms (0.47M params) to 1.12 ms (3.03M params) per position at batch 1**. A dense 4,672-way fully connected head cost 19.6M params and about 6x the latency.

These figures come from a 32-thread Xeon running ORT 1.32 and are an optimistic bound. The tournament box runs ORT 1.24.2 on 2 physical cores shared with the opponent, CPU only, 4 GiB, with a fresh process per game (`starter/README.md`). Even 3x slower, a 2M-param network allows thousands of evaluations within the roughly 5 s per move that a 180 s + 2 s clock gives over a typical game; Obscuro's games averaged 116.6 plies ([Zhang & Sandholm 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.01242v2)). Five advisors as heads on one shared trunk cost about one forward pass, while five separate networks cost five.

Training is the binding constraint. The only single-GPU deep-RL result in a chess-like imperfect-information game is the RBC agent: supervised pretraining, then PPO against past snapshots, with "a few days" on one M40 buying about +212 Elo and "no indication of convergence" ([Bertram et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02029)). Gumbel AlphaZero reached 9×9 Go strength in about 105M frames (about 8 h on one A100) but did not attempt full chess, and the Gumbel chess curves extend to about 1.6×10⁹ frames ([Koyamada et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.17503v4); [Danihelka et al. 2022](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2022/poster/6418)).

Raw simulation is cheap. Locally, python-chess sustained about **52k pseudo-legal plies/s per core**, which across 30 cores is on the order of 10¹¹ plies per day. Network inference and FoW observation encoding, not move generation, will set the pace. Five independently trained agents divide the budget by five, and that is before the empathic retraining the theory demands whenever the weights move.

Several engineering choices are well supported. **Exact legal-move masking** is available because a FoW player knows its own pseudo-legal moves, and masking beats invalid-action penalties decisively ([Huang & Ontañón](https://arxiv.org/html/2006.14171v3)). A **stack of about 20 past observations** matched LSTM/GRU accuracy in RBC at lower training cost ([Bertram et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02029)). A **privileged critic** that sees the true board during training and is discarded at deployment avoids the problem Suphx reported, where naive oracle distillation "did not work well" ([PerfectDou](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/e26f31de8b13ec569bf507e6ae2cd952-Supplemental-Conference.pdf); [Suphx](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.13590)). Finally, a **cheap opponent pool** snapshots the learner at 65% win rate and samples past versions with probability 1 − w_i/Σw_j ([Bertram et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.02029)).

Two things need checking before anyone relies on them. First, whether a Stockfish binary may be shipped within the course's 128 MiB upload and 4 GiB limits is unverified. Second, the only open Obscuro-style reimplementation, misty-chess, is GPL-3.0, and its pure-Python fallback is about 500x slower on the belief hot path ([PyPI misty-chess](https://pypi.org/project/misty-chess/)).

## Measuring alignment error is the novel contribution worth funding

The novelty is real but narrow. The research turned up **no paper that fits NNLS or CMA-ES weights over decomposed Q-heads for game control**, and **no application of reward decomposition or multi-advisor RL to two-player imperfect-information games**. The convergence results of Russell and Zimdars, Laroche and drQ all assume a single agent in a fixed environment, so whether empathic planning's advantage survives an adapting opponent is genuinely open ([Russell & Zimdars 2003](https://aima.eecs.berkeley.edu/~russell/papers/ml03-qdecomp.pdf); [Laroche et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1704.00756)).

The scientifically interesting deliverable is therefore not "a strong bot." It is the **first empirical measurement of Collina's market-alignment ε in an adversarial imperfect-information game**. Fit w in logit space on positions the principal itself reaches, report the sup and average residuals against outcomes (or against a determinized-engine reference value), and compare the learned combination with both the best single advisor (the Theorem 6 baseline) and the uniform average (the Section 6 baseline).

This question lives most naturally in the heuristic council. Its advisors are myopic, non-potential features, so their weights genuinely change the policy and ε is meaningful. In the theoretically sound deep-RL council, by contrast, potential-based heads become action-invariant and the weights matter only for scouting and for approximation error. The handoff note's proposed extension, advisors with *different beliefs* that each sample consistent boards in their own way, restores the private-information structure that Collina's information-substitutes theorem depends on. It is the most genuinely novel variant on the table.

The recommended modifications follow directly from the evidence. Put a hard king-capture safety filter and a visible-king-capture override in front of sampling. Replace the global temperature with Obscuro-style top-k mixing (k ≤ 3) gated on the value margin. Feed belief features, such as board-set statistics or sampled boards, rather than a single observation. Allow phase-dependent weights. Value scouting by the decision it changes rather than by entropy reduction. If deep-RL advisors are built, use a shared trunk with empathic expected-Sarsa targets under π_w and potential-based material, king-safety and pressure heads. Condition on w only where it matters, the scouting head and the approximate regime, and keep the terminal-win head out of the regressors. Throughout, report performance against an adaptive best-response opponent, not only win rate against a fixed pool.

The main risks are concrete. The ε fit could look excellent on a reference dataset and then fail on the positions the council steers itself into, which is the Goodhart pattern Skalse et al. predict. Empathic retraining every time CMA-ES moves w could consume the GPU budget. CMA-ES could overfit λ and w to a frozen opponent pool and collapse against an adaptive classmate. The material term could be baited by sacrifices against an invisible king. The empathic-versus-egocentric difference could vanish once a real adversary is present. That last outcome would be a legitimate negative result, not a failure, so the plan below treats it as reportable.

## Conclusion

The proposal's real contribution is a question, not an architecture: does "alignment through a weighted sum of misaligned objectives" survive contact with an adversary who watches the weights in action? Answering it cleanly means stripping out the confounds that sink the naive version. Selfish advisors make the sum optimistic about every policy at once, a global temperature turns randomness into noise rather than strategy, and missing belief tracking means even a perfect evaluation is applied to the wrong board. Once those confounds are removed, an unexpected point appears. The theoretically sound deep-RL council makes most of its weights inert, so Collina's ε is most meaningful, and most cheaply measured, in the humble heuristic council.

That reverses the natural investment order. Win the arena with the Stage 0 council, which is Texel tuning with a game-theoretic vocabulary. Spend the GPU weeks on the ε measurement and the empathic-versus-egocentric comparison, results that no one has published, rather than on hoping that five shaped-reward agents add up to a dark-chess player. Expect RL advisors to pay off as an evaluator inside search, and expect them to stay well short of Obscuro-class play.
